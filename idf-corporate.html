01 · The shift

Your frameworks rationed effort. Judgment is now the scarce part.

Sprints, WIP limits and estimation all assumed people’s effort was the limit. AI collapsed that effort. The question moves from “can we build it?” to “is it worth building, and did it work?”

What breaks

  • Hierarchy built on task routing
  • Middle management as a coordination layer
  • Sequential approval chains
  • Estimation as the filter for bad ideas
  • Silos that control access to execution
  • Status reporting as infrastructure

What gets scarcer

  • Outcome ownership
  • Strategic direction
  • Judgment at the four moments
  • Accountability held by a named person
  • Governance, which gains weight as execution speeds up

The four moments you govern

MomentThe questionWithout it
DirectionIs this worth doing?The wrong thing, built perfectly, fast
FitnessDoes it solve the problem we stated?Passing tests, failing customers
ExposureShould customers see it now?Risk arrives on someone else’s schedule
ConfirmationDid it work?Output counted as outcome, forever

You decide where to place them and how formal to make them; a regulated bank answers differently from a twelve-person team. Four has held up in the scenarios tested so far, which keeps it a working hypothesis.

Your old governance was partly a side effect of slowness. Someone read the diff because reading took forty minutes. Agents removed that friction, and the evidence it left.

02 · From strategy to intent

Strategy without a signal is a wish.

Leaders own three levels: strategy, objectives and business outcomes. Keep the format you already use; OKRs work. IDF asks only that each level is written down, has an observable signal and a named owner.

Leaders set the levels above the dashed boxes. Each dashed box is an intent, and several can serve one business outcome, each testing a different bet.

Portfolio rules

Fund intents

Teams and headcount follow. A funded intent has an owner, a signal and a window.

Trace or don’t fund

If an intent doesn’t trace up to a business outcome, don’t fund it.

Two cycles, no movement

The intent gets a Direction review before more execution.

Abandon in public

Resolve conflicts between outcomes before teams start. Expect some intents to close as abandoned.

03 · The org model

Any function can run on intents.

That is IDF’s working hypothesis. The evidence so far comes from software delivery, so treat it as untested outside it.

FinanceClose the books cleanlyAutomate reconciliationZero manual journal entries
OperationsResolve tickets fasterPriority-based routing90th percentile under 4 hours

What transforms

Managerstask routing → owning outcomes and closing intents
Specialistsdoing every instance → writing the standard agents read

What shifts

Meetingsstatus → decision
Reportingassembly → consumption
Approvalmilestones → judgment moments

What it costs

  • Senior attention, permanently. Judgment doesn’t scale.
  • Cover for people who reject finished work.
  • Friction you paid to remove, added back by choice.
  • Seeing which work didn’t work, without softening it.
04 · The information layer

Keep the ceremonies. Change what you bring.

Signals and delivery records accumulate as a by-product of execution. Nobody assembles them the week before a meeting.

WhoReadsWhen
Board · C-suiteObjectives and their signalsQuarterly, or when an objective closes
VP · BU leadBusiness outcomes and the intents serving themEach cycle
Outcome ownerIntent Log, review reports, Confirmation signalsContinuously
Delivery TeamReview reports, Iteration RecordsEach iteration

What each ceremony becomes

QBR

Decide which intents continue, change or close.

Sprint review

A Fitness review: someone uses the work as a customer would.

All-hands

Context and narrative on data people have already seen.

Board deck

Compiled from signals. Less assembly, same responsibility.

Retrospective

Decide what to change; the data is already there.

Regulated environments

Some ceremonies exist because the law requires a human signature. A change advisory board may still be mandatory. AI prepares the evidence, a named person accepts the liability, and the ceremony stays.

Leaders set the outcomes. Teams deliver against them.

If an intent can’t be traced to a business outcome, the work is unfunded in the strategic sense, even if it has budget.

Read this as hypotheses

Building IDF with IDF exposed a failure: a review function logged 13 passes in 13 cycles and caught nothing, because it had no independence. No controlled study stands behind the ladder or the four moments, and nobody has tested them at enterprise scale. Check each claim against your own organization.

The full argument, with its sources, costs and open questions, is in The Judgment Layer. IDF’s author has worked at CI&T since 2007 and built IDF to test these ideas in use. You don’t need IDF to use them.
